Biography

Maria Dulce Leitão Reis

This is a broad outline of my biography; much remains unsaid.

I am a poet, writer, and author. I contribute to anthologies, advocate for peace, and am a humanist.

“Poetry is an incredible force.”
Roots, childhood, and education.

I was born on May 4, 1954, in a small village in central Portugal. During my childhood, I spent several years in Angola—a country that has remained close to my heart and holds a cherished place in my memories.

I studied at the National High School (Liceu Nacional) in Guarda and at the Music Conservatory in Covilhã.

Early public life and professional experience.

I took part in two documentaries filmed in the Serra da Estrela mountains. These films promoted the region and winter sports—specifically skiing—and were screened in cinemas across the country.

From 1973 onwards, I spent 15 years working in the accounting department of a large company operating in the tourism and finance sectors. Later, I became an entrepreneur in the restaurant and fashion industries.

Literary beginnings and the spark of creativity.

I began writing poetry at a very young age; however, I often destroyed my early poems for fear that others might read them. I believe I inherited my poetic sensibility from my maternal grandfather—a man I remember as being exceptionally generous, kind, and wise.

My Portuguese teacher recognised my talent and encouraged my writing. She also sparked my interest in acting, after seeing me perform the role of Maria in the play Frei Luís de Sousa. Although the circumstances of my upbringing—and the limited opportunities available to girls in rural communities—prevented me from pursuing a career in acting, I channelled my creative energy into other goals and projects.

Later, I contributed poems and short stories to my children’s school newspaper and recited poetry on a local radio station.

Life and Work in England

I lived in England between 2006 and 2020. During that time, I worked for a multinational electronics engineering company that manufactured electronic components. I also worked as a waitress and an assistant chef in restaurants, successfully balancing various professional responsibilities.

I look back on my time in England with fondness; while I admit it was difficult at first, I eventually came to feel at home. I never felt like an outsider, as I was always treated with respect by the English people.

Family Care, Bereavement, and Resilience

I returned to Portugal in March 2020 to care for my parents—just before the restrictions imposed by the pandemic made returning much more difficult. My father passed away in December 2020 at the age of 93, and my mother passed away in August 2021 at the age of 88.

These were particularly difficult years, as I was also caring for my daughter, who was ill. Consequently, my writing and publishing activities slowed down, and I have not published any further books since 2020.

Anthologies and International Publications

I am a co-author of 70 anthologies, in Portuguese and English, covering poetry and prose (articles, chronicles, essays, allegories, and short stories).

My work has been featured in international literary magazines, blogs, and newspapers, including the inaugural issue of Poetry Planet Magazine. My books are available through online platforms such as Amazon, Bookemon, and Lulu, as well as other booksellers.

In 2023, I participated in the book Hyperpoem—a project by Alexander Kabishev in Russia—which entered the Guinness World Records.

I was interviewed by New York-based Spillwords Press for their "Featured Authors" section. August 14, 2021.

Literary Recognition and Publishing Achievements

The work Tree of Love and Serenity was distinguished by Bookemon USA as the best poetry book of 2019.

In 2020, I was the best-selling author for the Philippine publisher Poetry Planet on Amazon. I have received other literary awards and distinctions, which I share via my Facebook presence.

Peace, Humanitarian Projects, and Community Service

I have been named an Ambassador for Peace by several international cultural organisations, with the first such appointment taking place in 2018.

I contributed to the work Let There Be Peace, produced by the World Institute for Peace in Nigeria—a book aimed at promoting international peace which was reportedly distributed to governments, libraries, and schools in over 50 countries. In 2020, I also participated in a UK-based project, The Book of Hope, created to support the National Health Service (NHS) during the COVID-19 pandemic.

I regularly donate clothing and footwear to charitable causes.

I am firmly opposed to injustice, inequality, discrimination, and violence in all their forms.

Current Writing Projects and Personal Interests

I currently have four other individual books in preparation, as well as material for further publications.

Beyond literature, I enjoy reading, Sudoku, crossword puzzles, certain smartphone games, photography, and gardening. My love for nature is deeply rooted in the conviction that compassion, love, and peace can help create a more welcoming and balanced world.

The Role of Poetry in My Life

I am involved with several poetry groups on Facebook, serving as an Administrator, Moderator, or Advisor. I am also the founder of the group Art Poetry Nook and manage seven Facebook pages, in addition to my personal profile.

For me, writing, is simultaneously an artistic expression and a personal source of meaning. My life’s journey—spanning different countries, careers, family responsibilities, and creative projects—has shaped the value I place on resilience and human connection.

In short, I would say that poetry is an incredible force.

Values and humanity

Writing beyond oneself.

Maria's relationship with writing extends beyond artistic expression. She sees it as a way of communicating ideas, encouraging reflection and bringing people together.

She has been appointed an Ambassador for Peace by several international cultural organisations, with her first appointment dating to 2018.

Her contributions include the international publication Let There Be Peace, associated with the World Institute for Peace in Nigeria, and participation in The Book of Hope, a UK project supporting the NHS during the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020.

Her humanitarian outlook is reflected in her concern for inequality, discrimination, injustice and the suffering of others.

For Maria, compassion, love and peace are not abstract ideals, but values to be practised in everyday life.

Today, she continues to write and prepare new individual publications, while preserving and sharing the work created throughout her life.
